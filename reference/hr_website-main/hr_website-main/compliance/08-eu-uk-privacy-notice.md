## 4. Special Category Data (GDPR Article 9)

HR Ops's Platform is not designed to process special category personal data (health data, biometric data, racial or ethnic origin, political opinions, religious beliefs, trade union membership, genetic data, sex life or sexual orientation data).

Customers must not upload special category data unless:
- They have a valid legal basis under Article 9(2) GDPR
- They have disclosed this to HR Ops in writing
- A special category data addendum to the DPA has been agreed

If special category data is inadvertently uploaded, HR Ops will delete it and notify the customer.

## 5. Your GDPR Rights

As an EU/UK data subject, you have the following rights. All requests should be sent to privacy@hrops.io and will be responded to within **30 days** (extendable by a further 60 days for complex requests, with notice):

### Right of access (Article 15)
You have the right to receive a copy of the personal data HR Ops holds about you, along with information about how it is processed, who it is shared with, and how long it is retained.

### Right to rectification (Article 16)
You have the right to correct inaccurate personal data and to complete incomplete data.

### Right to erasure (Article 17) â€” "right to be forgotten"
You have the right to request deletion of your personal data where:
- The data is no longer necessary for the purpose for which it was collected
- You withdraw consent (where consent was the legal basis)
- You object to processing and there are no overriding legitimate grounds
- The data has been unlawfully processed

**Limitations:** Erasure may be declined where data must be retained for legal compliance (e.g. financial records, audit obligations under applicable law). HR Ops will explain any such limitation in writing.

### Right to restriction (Article 18)
You have the right to restrict processing of your data â€” meaning HR Ops will store it but not process it further â€” where:
- You contest the accuracy of the data (during verification)
- Processing is unlawful but you prefer restriction over erasure
- HR Ops no longer needs the data but you need it for a legal claim
- You have objected to processing pending verification of legitimate grounds

### Right to data portability (Article 20)
Where processing is based on consent or contract and is carried out by automated means, you have the right to receive your personal data in a structured, commonly used, machine-readable format (JSON or CSV) and to transmit it to another controller.

### Right to object (Article 21)
You have the right to object, at any time, to processing of your personal data based on legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f)) or for direct marketing purposes. On receiving an objection to direct marketing, HR Ops will stop immediately. For other legitimate interest processing, HR Ops will stop unless it can demonstrate compelling legitimate grounds that override your interests.

### Rights related to automated decision-making (Article 22)
You have the right not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing that produces legal or similarly significant effects.

HR Ops's AI screening feature produces recommendations, not final decisions â€” a human must review every AI verdict before it has any effect on a candidate's application. This satisfies Article 22 requirements. However, if you believe an automated decision has been made about your application without human review, please contact privacy@hrops.io and we will investigate immediately.

### Right to lodge a complaint
You have the right to lodge a complaint with your national supervisory authority. A list of EU supervisory authorities is available at edpb.europa.eu/about-edpb/about-edpb/members_en. The UK supervisory authority is the Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk).

## 6. International Data Transfers (GDPR Chapter V)

Personal data of EU/EEA data subjects is transferred from the EEA to India (where HR Ops is established). India is not currently the subject of an EU adequacy decision under GDPR Article 45.

HR Ops relies on **Standard Contractual Clauses (SCCs)** â€” specifically, European Commission Decision 2021/914, Module 2 (Controller to Processor) â€” as the transfer mechanism. The SCCs are incorporated into the Data Processing Agreement.

**Transfer impact assessment:** HR Ops has conducted a Transfer Impact Assessment (TIA) for the EEA-to-India transfer. Key findings:
- Indian law (including the DPDP Act) provides meaningful protections for personal data
- HR Ops has not received and does not anticipate receiving government access requests relating to EU data
- Supplementary measures in place include: encryption at rest and in transit, access controls, and data minimisation by architecture

The full TIA is available to Enterprise customers on request.

**For UK data subjects:** HR Ops relies on the **UK International Data Transfer Agreement (IDTA)** or the UK Addendum to the EU SCCs as the transfer mechanism for transfers from the UK to India.

## 7. EU AI Act Obligations

HR Ops's AI resume screening feature is a **high-risk AI system** under Annex III, point 4(a) of the EU AI Act (AI systems used for recruitment or selection of natural persons, in particular for advertising vacancies, screening or filtering applications, evaluating candidates in the course of interviews or tests).

HR Ops meets the following EU AI Act obligations:

### Risk management system (Article 9)
HR Ops maintains a documented risk management system for its AI resume screening feature, covering:
- Identification of known and foreseeable risks
- Evaluation of risks arising from use, misuse, and interaction with data
- Residual risk assessment after mitigation

### Data governance (Article 10)
Training data used for HR Ops's AI models is subject to:
- Relevance and representativeness checks
- Bias detection across protected characteristics (gender, age, geographic region)
- Exclusion of special category data from training sets

### Technical documentation (Article 11)
Technical documentation for the AI resume screening system is maintained and available to Enterprise customers and relevant authorities on request: security@hrops.io

### Transparency to users (Article 13)
Employers and agencies using HR Ops are provided with clear information about:
- The capabilities and limitations of the AI screening feature
- The level of accuracy and the metrics used
- Any foreseeable circumstances in which the system may fail or produce unexpected outputs
- Human oversight requirements

### Human oversight (Article 14)
The Platform is designed to require human review before any AI screening verdict affects a candidate. HR Ops will notify customers if any configuration would bypass this requirement.

### Accuracy and robustness (Article 15)
HR Ops's AI screening feature is tested for accuracy and robustness. Bias testing results are published at hrops.io/ai-transparency.

### Transparency to candidates (Article 50)
Employers using HR Ops to evaluate candidates are required to:
- Inform candidates that AI tools are used in the evaluation process
- Provide candidates with meaningful information about the logic involved

HR Ops provides a template candidate disclosure notice in Platform Settings. This template satisfies Article 50 requirements.

**EU AI Act status:** The EU AI Act's high-risk AI provisions apply from August 2026. HR Ops is implementing full compliance ahead of this deadline.
