## 1. Overview

India's **Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023** ("**DPDP Act**") came into force on 11 August 2023. It is India's first comprehensive personal data protection law and establishes a framework for the processing of digital personal data, the rights of data principals, and the obligations of data fiduciaries and data processors.

HR Ops Technologies Private Limited ("**HR Ops**") is committed to full compliance with the DPDP Act in all its operations. This statement describes how HR Ops meets the obligations of the Act — both as a **Data Fiduciary** (for data HR Ops controls) and as a **Data Processor** (for data processed on behalf of employer and agency customers).

This statement should be read alongside our Privacy Policy, Data Processing Agreement, and Terms of Service.

## 2. Roles Under the DPDP Act

### 2.1 HR Ops as Data Fiduciary

HR Ops is a **Data Fiduciary** for:
- Personal data of its own employees
- Personal data of account holders, billing contacts, and admin users of the Platform
- Personal data of website visitors to hrops.io
- Any personal data processed for HR Ops's own business purposes (marketing, fraud prevention, platform improvement)

As a Data Fiduciary, HR Ops determines the purpose and means of processing this data and is directly responsible for compliance.

### 2.2 HR Ops as Data Processor

HR Ops is a **Data Processor** for:
- Candidate personal data uploaded to or collected through the Platform by employer and agency customers
- Employee data entered into the Platform by customers (e.g. details of hiring managers and interviewers)
- Any other personal data where the customer (employer or agency) determines the purpose and means of processing

As a Data Processor, HR Ops processes this data only on the instructions of the customer (Data Fiduciary) and in accordance with the Data Processing Agreement.

### 2.3 Significant Data Fiduciary readiness

The DPDP Act empowers the Central Government to designate certain Data Fiduciaries as **Significant Data Fiduciaries** (SDFs) based on volume, sensitivity, or risk of data processed. HR Ops monitors developments in SDF designation and maintains readiness to comply with additional obligations (Data Protection Officer appointment, DPIA, algorithmic audit, data localisation restrictions) if designated.

## 3. Lawful Basis for Processing

The DPDP Act requires that personal data be processed on a lawful basis. HR Ops relies on the following bases:

| Processing activity | Data subjects | Lawful basis |
|---|---|---|
| Account creation and management | Account holders | Contract — necessary to provide the Platform |
| Billing and invoicing | Billing contacts | Contract; Legal obligation (tax law) |
| Candidate processing through the Platform | Candidates | Consent (obtained by the employer/agency) and/or Legitimate use as permitted by law |
| Platform analytics (anonymised) | All users | Legitimate use |
| Security and fraud prevention | All users | Legitimate use |
| Marketing to prospective customers | Leads and prospects | Consent |
| Responding to support queries | Users | Contract / Legitimate use |
| Legal compliance and regulatory reporting | As applicable | Legal obligation |

### 3.1 Consent management

Where consent is the lawful basis, HR Ops ensures that consent is:
- **Free** — not bundled with unrelated conditions; no detriment for refusal of non-essential processing
- **Specific** — obtained for a defined and clearly stated purpose
- **Informed** — accompanied by a clear notice explaining the data, purpose, and data principal's rights
- **Unambiguous** — obtained through an affirmative action (not pre-ticked boxes, silence, or inaction)

Consent records are maintained (timestamp, purpose, version of notice) and can be produced on request.

### 3.2 Notice requirement (Section 5 DPDP Act)

Before or at the time of collecting personal data, HR Ops provides a notice in clear and plain language specifying:
- The personal data being collected
- The purpose for which it is being processed
- How data principals can exercise their rights
- How to raise a grievance

For employer/agency customers managing candidate data: HR Ops provides a **Candidate Privacy Notice** template in Platform Settings. Customers are obligated to provide this (or equivalent) notice to candidates.

## 4. Data Principal Rights

The DPDP Act grants the following rights to data principals (individuals whose personal data is processed). HR Ops facilitates exercise of these rights:

### 4.1 Right to information (Section 11)

Data principals have the right to know:
- Whether their personal data is being processed
- A summary of the personal data being processed
- The identities of all Data Processors and recipients

**How to exercise:** Email privacy@hrops.io. Response within 30 days.

For candidates whose data is managed by an employer/agency customer: contact the employer/agency directly. HR Ops will assist in forwarding requests.

### 4.2 Right to correction and erasure (Section 12)

Data principals have the right to:
- Correct inaccurate or misleading personal data
- Complete incomplete personal data
- Erase personal data that is no longer necessary for the purpose for which it was collected

**How to exercise:**
- HR Ops account holders and users: update data in Platform Settings, or email privacy@hrops.io
- Candidates: contact the employer or agency who processed your application. HR Ops will facilitate the request upon instruction from the customer.
- Response time: within 30 days; HR Ops will confirm completion

**Limitations:** HR Ops may decline erasure where retention is required by law (e.g. tax records, audit logs required under applicable statutes). Where a request is declined, HR Ops will provide written reasons.

### 4.3 Right to nominate (Section 14)

Data principals may nominate another individual to exercise DPDP Act rights on their behalf in the event of the data principal's death or incapacity. To register a nominee: email privacy@hrops.io with the nominee's details and a signed declaration.

### 4.4 Right to grievance redressal (Section 13)

Data principals may raise grievances about HR Ops's data processing. Every grievance will be:
- Acknowledged within **72 hours**
- Substantively responded to within **30 days**
- Escalated to a senior data protection contact if not resolved at first instance

**Grievance contact:** privacy@hrops.io

If a grievance is not resolved satisfactorily, data principals may approach the **Data Protection Board of India** (once operational).

## 5. Data Fiduciary Obligations

### 5.1 Purpose limitation (Section 6)

Personal data is processed only for the purposes for which it was collected. HR Ops does not use candidate data submitted for one job application for any other purpose without fresh consent. HR Ops does not process customer data for any purpose beyond delivering the Platform and related services.

### 5.2 Data minimisation

HR Ops processes only the personal data necessary for the stated purpose. Architectural measures enforce minimisation:
- Document files (resumes, assessment materials) are not stored on HR Ops servers — only extracted text required for screening is processed
- AI screening uses only role-relevant attributes; HR Ops actively tests its models to identify and mitigate use of irrelevant attributes

### 5.3 Accuracy

HR Ops takes reasonable steps to ensure the accuracy of personal data. Users are able to correct their own data through Platform settings. Candidate data accuracy is the responsibility of the candidate and the employer; HR Ops provides tools for correction.

### 5.4 Storage limitation

Personal data is retained only for the period necessary for the purpose. HR Ops's retention schedule is published in the Privacy Policy. Automated deletion is enforced for data past its retention period.

### 5.5 Security safeguards

HR Ops implements appropriate technical and organisational measures to protect personal data. Full details are in the Security Policy (hrops.io/legal/security). Key measures:
- AES-256 encryption at rest; TLS 1.3 in transit
- SOC 2 Type II certified security programme
- ISO/IEC 27001-aligned ISMS
- Annual penetration testing
- Immutable audit log

### 5.6 Accountability and record-keeping

HR Ops maintains records of:
- Categories of personal data processed
- Purposes of processing
- Data Processor engagements (sub-processor list)
- Consent records
- Data principal rights requests and responses
- Security incidents

## 6. Cross-Border Data Transfers

The DPDP Act permits cross-border transfers of personal data to countries notified by the Central Government. Until a formal whitelist is published:

- **Default:** All personal data of Indian data principals is processed and stored in India (AWS ap-south-1, Mumbai). No default cross-border transfer occurs.
- **Sub-processors outside India:** Where HR Ops uses sub-processors located outside India (e.g. Sentry for error monitoring, Intercom for support chat), only the minimum necessary technical data is transferred, and such sub-processors are bound by data processing agreements with equivalent protections.
- **BYOK AI:** Where customers use their own AI API keys (BYOK), candidate data is transmitted to the customer's chosen AI provider under the customer's API key. Customers are responsible for ensuring this transfer complies with applicable law.

HR Ops will update this section as cross-border transfer rules are notified under the DPDP Act.

## 7. Processing of Children's Data

The DPDP Act imposes strict obligations on processing personal data of children (under 18 years).

HR Ops:
- Does not knowingly collect or process personal data of children
- Does not target the Platform at children
- Requires employer and agency customers to confirm that job applicants are 18 years or older before processing their data through the Platform
- If HR Ops becomes aware that a child's data has been processed, it will immediately delete that data and notify the relevant customer

Customers must not use the Platform to run hiring processes for roles legally open to persons under 18 without specific written agreement and appropriate safeguards.

## 8. AI Processing and the DPDP Act

HR Ops's AI resume screening feature involves automated processing of personal data. In the DPDP Act context:

- **Automated decisions:** HR Ops's AI produces recommendations, not final decisions. A human must review and act on AI verdicts. This ensures that no consequential decision about a data principal is made solely by automated means.
- **Transparency:** Candidates are informed (via the Candidate Privacy Notice) that AI tools are used in the screening process.
- **Right to explanation:** Candidates may request a plain-English explanation of an AI screening verdict by contacting the employer or emailing privacy@hrops.io.
- **Bias testing:** HR Ops tests its AI models for demographic bias (gender, age, geographic location) and publishes results at hrops.io/ai-transparency.

## 9. Data Processor Obligations (When Acting as Processor)

When HR Ops acts as a Data Processor for employer/agency customers, it:

- Processes personal data only on documented instructions from the customer
- Ensures all HR Ops personnel with access to customer data are bound by confidentiality obligations
- Implements appropriate technical and organisational security measures (see Security Policy)
- Does not engage sub-processors without the customer's prior consent (general consent is given via the DPA and sub-processor list)
- Assists the customer to fulfil data principal rights requests
- Deletes or returns all personal data on termination of the customer relationship (within 90 days)
- Provides all information necessary to demonstrate compliance
- Notifies the customer of any security incident affecting the customer's personal data within 72 hours

## 10. Grievance Redressal

HR Ops has designated a data protection contact for grievance redressal under Section 13 of the DPDP Act.

**Data Protection Contact:**  
privacy@hrops.io

**Grievance process:**
1. Submit your grievance by email to privacy@hrops.io
2. Include: your name, contact details, description of the grievance, and what resolution you are seeking
3. HR Ops will acknowledge within 72 hours
4. HR Ops will provide a substantive response within 30 days
5. If unresolved, HR Ops will escalate internally and revert within a further 14 days
6. If still unresolved, you may approach the Data Protection Board of India

HR Ops is committed to resolving all data protection grievances fairly, promptly, and at no cost to the data principal.
